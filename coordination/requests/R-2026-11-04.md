### Q1. Closest to a non-tautological recurrence: the connected-join route

**Connected-join route (B). This is the strongest surviving approach.** It has two Lean theorems.
- **K7, `labeledWinningCount_connected_expansion` (PR #34).** This is the free moment–cumulant relation, W_n(q) = Σ over noncrossing π of Π κ_|B|(q). Given the κ's, it yields the recurrence m_t = Σ_s κ_s [z^{t−s}] M(z)^s.
- **K8, `connectedGamePolynomial_natDegree_le` and `connectedGamePolynomial_coeff_zero` (PR #42).** κ_s(q) has no constant term, and its q^j coefficient c(s,j) vanishes unless s ≥ 5j.

**K7 and K8 together are a genuinely non-tautological uniform statement.** W_n(q) for *every* q is determined by the counts over at most max(1, ⌊n/5⌋) letters.
- I tested this on 2026-11-18 (`research/all_alphabets_20261118/`).
- From the 1-, 2- and 3-letter data alone it reproduces every published OEIS term for q = 4, 5, 6 and 8, through n = 19.
- It gives W_n(q) as an explicit polynomial in q for n ≤ 20, together with new terms.
- So the theorems have real predictive content.

**What it does not give is a recurrence for fixed q.** That would need an independent description of the array c(s,j), indexed by the excess e = s − 5j.
- e = 0 is exact, but only through the counts A_j = 5, 35, 415, 6455, 118195, 2413185. These come from enumeration; I know of no closed form or recurrence for them, and that is the first real gap.
- e = 1 is exact on paper (K10).
- e = 2 is partial (K11).
- Nothing yet links the layers.

**Fusion-support route (A).** It has the exact semantics: F1 in `FusionStack.lean` and F3 in `FusionHorizon.lean`. It is the engine behind every term to n = 28 and behind the growth bound E1. **As a recurrence it is tautological.** It is a transfer computation over support sets whose number grows exponentially: about 29M states at n = 26 (`count26.json`).
- No bounded sufficient statistic is known, and two things block the natural candidates.
  - F3's non-lumpability witness: reversal is not a transition automorphism.
  - The finite-summary counterexample AAABBABCCCBC versus AAABABBCCCBC: these two have equal summaries, yet one wins with C appended and the other loses.
- In my view those block any finite-state compression of the obvious kind, though they do not prove one is impossible.

**Map between the two routes: not found, and not attempted in earnest.**
- The two routes measure different objects: sets of reachable stack residues of a prefix, versus Möbius-weighted sums over whole-word patterns.
- A map would have to send prefix supports to partial noncrossing structures.
- I record this as open. I am not claiming an obstruction.

**Recommendation.**
- Use route B for structure and publication.
- Use route A for terms and asymptotics.
- Stop presenting either as a recurrence.

### Q2. Smallest unproved general lemma

**(a) Smallest lemma already true on paper but not in Lean: the first-excess coefficient law (K10, PR #50).**

```lean
theorem ncConnected_firstExcess_coefficient {n : Nat} (p : ColorPattern n)
    (hc : NCConnected p) (hk : 2 ≤ Nat.card (Quotient p))
    (hne : winningJoinCoefficient n p ≠ 0)
    (heq : n = 5 * Nat.card (Quotient p) + 1) :
    winningJoinCoefficient n p = -1
```

The class profile (6,5,…,5) is forced by `ncConnected_nonzero_every_class_min_five` together with `heq`, so it need not be a hypothesis.
